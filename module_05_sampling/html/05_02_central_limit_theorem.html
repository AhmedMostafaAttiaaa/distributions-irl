05_02 The Central Limit Theorem

Averages and sums of many independent pieces look Normal, whatever the pieces look like.

Key ideas

$Z_n=\dfrac{\bar X_n-\mu}{\sigma/\sqrt n}\to N(0,1)$ for iid $X_i$ with finite variance; so $\bar X_n\approx N(\mu,\sigma^2/n)$ and $S_n\approx N(n\mu,n\sigma^2)$. Speed (Berry-Esseen): error $\le\dfrac{0.4748\,\rho}{\sigma^3\sqrt n}$, mostly set by the skewness $\gamma$ of the parent; first correction $-\dfrac{\gamma}{6\sqrt n}(z^2-1)\varphi(z)$. It fails for infinite variance (Cauchy, Pareto with $\alpha\le2$) and is slow for heavy skew.

In plain words

  • The shape of the pieces is forgotten; only their mean and spread survive when you add many of them. Try the Uniform and the Two-humped parents at n = 5.
  • Skewness sets the speed. Exponential needs a few dozen draws; Bernoulli(0.01) or Lognormal(s = 2) need thousands (watch the Q-Q plot bend).
  • The centre converges first, the tails last (Example 2: the Normal overstates the false alarm rate of a bounded parent by three times).
  • "n = 30" is a rule of thumb, not a law (Example 3: with heavy skew the 95 % interval misses far too often, always on the same side).

Where it is used

Statistics

Confidence intervals, z and t tests, control charts, polling margins, asymptotics of maximum-likelihood estimators.

Data science

A/B test statistics, aggregated metrics such as daily revenue or latency totals, sample-size planning.

ML and AI

Gaussian noise of mini-batch gradients, wide networks as Gaussian processes, Laplace approximations of posteriors.
