06_06 Multiple Testing: Bonferroni, Holm and Benjamini-Hochberg

Why many tests produce false discoveries, and how to control the family-wise error rate or the false discovery rate.

Key ideas

FWER $=P(V\ge1)$, FDR $=E[V/\max(R,1)]$. Bonferroni: reject if $p_i\le\alpha/m$. Holm: reject $H_{(1)},\dots,H_{(k-1)}$ where $k$ is the first with $p_{(k)}>\frac\alpha{m-k+1}$. Benjamini-Hochberg: reject $H_{(1)},\dots,H_{(k)}$ with $k$ the largest such that $p_{(k)}\le\frac km\alpha$; FDR $\le\frac{m_0}m\alpha$. Uncorrected FWER $=1-(1-\alpha)^m$. BH adjusted: $\tilde p_{(i)}=\min_{j\ge i}\frac mjp_{(j)}$. Tukey HSD: $|\bar x_i-\bar x_j|>q_{\alpha;k,N-k}\,s/\sqrt n$.

In plain words

  • More tests, more luck: 20 tests of nothing find "something" 64 % of the time; use the simulator to see it for any number.
  • Bonferroni and Holm keep the chance of any false claim at 5 %, at a price in power.
  • Benjamini-Hochberg keeps the share of false claims among your discoveries near 5 % and finds far more real effects.
  • Pick the family of tests before looking, and one pre-specified primary test needs no correction at all.

Where it is used

Statistics

Genomics and imaging (FDR), clinical trials with several endpoints (Holm), post-hoc comparisons after ANOVA (Tukey).

Data science

A/B tests with many metrics and segments, drift monitoring on many features, any scan over many slices of a dataset.

ML and AI

Feature screening, choosing the best of many models or hyperparameters (winner's curse), fairness checks across many subgroups.
