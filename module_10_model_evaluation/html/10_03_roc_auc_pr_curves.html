10_03 ROC and Precision-Recall Curves, and AUC as a Probability

Every operating point of a scorer at once: the ROC curve, AUC as the chance that a positive outranks a negative, and why PR curves expose rare events.

Key ideas

$\mathrm{AUC}=P(S_1>S_0)=\frac{R_1-n_1(n_1+1)/2}{n_1n_0}$; Gaussian: $\Phi(d'/\sqrt2)$, or $\Phi(\mu/\sqrt{1+\sigma_1^2})$ with $S_0\sim N(0,1)$, $S_1\sim N(\mu,\sigma_1^2)$. Gini $=2\mathrm{AUC}-1$, KS $=\max(\mathrm{TPR}-\mathrm{FPR})$. ROC slope $=f_1(t)/f_0(t)$. Precision $=\frac{\pi\mathrm{TPR}}{\pi\mathrm{TPR}+(1-\pi)\mathrm{FPR}}$; AP chance level $=\pi$.

In plain words

  • The ROC curve shows all thresholds at once; the AUC is the chance that a random positive outranks a random negative.
  • AUC ignores prevalence and calibration (any increasing transformation of the scores leaves it unchanged).
  • The PR curve falls when positives are rare even though the ROC curve does not move.
  • Two scorers can share an AUC and still differ at the thresholds you can afford.

Where it is used

Statistics

Diagnostic test evaluation, the Mann-Whitney rank-sum test, the KS statistic, signal detection theory.

Data science

Credit-scoring Gini and KS, fraud and churn ranking, partial AUC for capacity-limited reviews.

ML and AI

AUROC of safety, toxicity, anomaly and out-of-distribution detectors; average precision for retrieval and detection.
