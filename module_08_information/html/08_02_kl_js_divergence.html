08_02 KL Divergence and Jensen-Shannon Divergence

How far apart two distributions are: extra surprise, asymmetry, the symmetric JS divergence and drift monitoring.

Key ideas

$\mathrm{KL}(p\|q)=\sum p\ln\frac pq=H(p,q)-H(p)\ge0$. $\mathrm{JS}=\frac12\mathrm{KL}(p\|m)+\frac12\mathrm{KL}(q\|m)\le\ln2$, $m=\frac{p+q}2$. $\mathrm{PSI}=\mathrm{KL}(p\|q)+\mathrm{KL}(q\|p)$. Gaussians: $\ln\frac{\sigma_2}{\sigma_1}+\frac{\sigma_1^2+(\mu_1-\mu_2)^2}{2\sigma_2^2}-\frac12$. G-test: $2n\,\mathrm{KL}(\hat p\|p^0)\sim\chi^2_{K-1}$. Pinsker: $\mathrm{TV}\le\sqrt{\mathrm{KL}/2}$.

In plain words

  • KL is the extra surprise of using the wrong model; it is zero only when the model is right.
  • It is not symmetric: a model that is too narrow costs far more than one that is too wide (when the truth is the wide one).
  • JS compares each with the average, so it is symmetric and capped at $\ln2$.
  • Measured KL has a noise floor of about $(K-1)/(2n)$; compare it with that before calling something drift.

Two Gaussians: KL in both directions and JS

Move the parameters of $p$ and $q$. KL is not symmetric (try a narrow $q$); JS is symmetric and never exceeds $\ln2$.

Worked example: is the live data drifting?

Training shares $p$ and live shares $q$ over five income bands; $n$ live applicants. $G=2n\,\mathrm{KL}(\hat q\|p)$ is compared with $\chi^2_4$. The noise floor $(K-1)/(2n)$ is what a no-drift sample would show.

3D view: KL to the standard normal

$\mathrm{KL}(N(\mu,\sigma^2)\|N(0,1))$ over the mean and standard deviation: zero at $(0,1)$, quadratic in the mean shift. Plotly is loaded from a CDN only on this page.

Where it is used

Statistics

Likelihood-ratio (G) tests, AIC and model selection, Fisher information, information gain in Bayesian updating.

Data science

Data and concept drift monitoring (PSI, KL, JS), model comparison and feature distribution checks before deployment.

ML and AI

VAEs, variational inference, distillation, PPO and RLHF KL penalties, t-SNE and UMAP, GAN theory.
