09_03 Naive Bayes: a Spam Filter from Bayes' Rule

Add up the evidence word by word: multinomial, Bernoulli and Gaussian naive Bayes on the real SMS Spam Collection.

Key ideas

$\ln\frac{P(s\mid x)}{P(h\mid x)}=\ln\frac{P(s)}{P(h)}+\sum_jx_j\ln\frac{\theta_{js}}{\theta_{jh}}$, $\hat\theta_{jc}=\frac{N_{jc}+\alpha}{N_c+\alpha V}$ (Dirichlet posterior mean). Decision: spam if log-odds $>t$, $t=\ln(C_{FP}/C_{FN})$ if calibrated. Gaussian NB: $\sum_j\ln N(x_j;\mu_{jc},\sigma_{jc}^2)$. Log-odds of independent feature groups add.

In plain words

  • Each word is a vote for spam or ham; the message score is the prior plus the sum of the votes.
  • Smoothing is a prior: pretend every word was seen $\alpha$ times in each class.
  • Great at ranking, bad at probabilities: correlated words are counted as independent evidence.
  • Set the threshold from the costs of the two kinds of error, on held-out data.

Tick the words that appear in a message (weights are the real $\ln(\theta_{\text{spam}}/\theta_{\text{ham}})$ of the model trained on 4,459 SMS messages). The score is the prior log-odds plus the sum of the votes.

Worked example: choosing the threshold from the costs

Test-set scores of 974 ham and 141 spam messages (bins of 5 log-odds units). Move the threshold, or set a false-positive/false-negative cost ratio and let Bayes' rule choose $t=\ln(\text{ratio})$ (valid for calibrated scores; these are over-confident, so also try other values).

Where it is used

Statistics

Generative classification and discriminant analysis, Bayesian networks, likelihood-ratio decision rules.

Data science

Spam and abuse filters, sentiment and topic classification, quick baselines for high-dimensional counts, cost-sensitive thresholds.

ML and AI

NLP baselines, generative against discriminative models, the factorised likelihoods behind mixture models, VAEs and language models.
