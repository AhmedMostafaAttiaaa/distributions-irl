09_02 Generalised Linear Models: Logistic, Poisson and Gamma Regression

A linear score, the right distribution and a link: odds ratios, rate ratios, exposure, overdispersion and IRLS from scratch.

Key ideas

GLM: $y\sim$ exponential family, $g(\mu)=x^\top\beta$. Logit: $e^{\beta}=$ odds ratio. Log: $e^\beta=$ rate ratio, offset $\ln e$. IRLS: $\beta\leftarrow(X^\top WX)^{-1}X^\top Wz$, $z=\eta+\frac{y-\mu}{d\mu/d\eta}$, $W=\frac{(d\mu/d\eta)^2}{V(\mu)}$. $\mathrm{Var}(\hat\beta)=\phi(X^\top WX)^{-1}$. Overdispersion: $\hat\phi=\chi^2/(n-p)>1$ $\Rightarrow$ negative binomial, $V=\mu+\alpha\mu^2$.

In plain words

  • A coefficient multiplies something: the odds (logistic) or the rate (Poisson), by $e^{\beta}$ per unit.
  • The link keeps predictions legal: probabilities in $[0,1]$, rates above zero.
  • IRLS is repeated weighted least squares, and usually settles in 4 to 8 rounds.
  • If counts are more scattered than Poisson allows, the intervals are too narrow; use the negative binomial.

Logistic regression: move the line, watch the likelihood

300 simulated visitors (feature $x$, outcome 0/1). Choose the intercept and slope: the log-likelihood is shown next to its maximum, reached by IRLS (Newton's method) in a few iterations.

Worked example: claim rates with exposure

$\ln\lambda=\beta_0+0.6\,\text{young}+0.3\,\text{urban}+\ln(\text{exposure})$ with base $\beta_0=-0.7$. The rate ratio is $e^{\beta}$. With an extra-scatter parameter $\alpha$ the claim count is negative binomial instead of Poisson.

Where it is used

Statistics

Logistic regression in medicine and social science (odds ratios), Poisson and negative binomial incidence models, Gamma and Tweedie GLMs for insurance.

Data science

Conversion, churn and default scores; claim counts and severities with exposure; explainable baselines before boosting.

ML and AI

Logistic and softmax regression as one-layer networks; the activation-loss pairs of output layers; calibration and GAMs.
