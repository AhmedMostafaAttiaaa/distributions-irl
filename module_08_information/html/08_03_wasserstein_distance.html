08_03 The Wasserstein Distance

The cost of moving one distribution onto another: earth mover's distance, optimal transport and the GAN motivation.

Key ideas

$W_p=(\inf_\gamma E|X-Y|^p)^{1/p}$. 1D: $W_1=\int|F-G|dx=\int_0^1|F^{-1}-G^{-1}|du$. Gaussians: $W_2^2=(\mu_1-\mu_2)^2+(\sigma_1-\sigma_2)^2$. Shift: $W_p=|c|$. Duality: $W_1=\sup_{\mathrm{Lip}\le1}E_\mu f-E_\nu f$. Disjoint supports: $\mathrm{JS}=\ln2$, $\mathrm{KL}=\infty$, $W_1=$ the gap.

In plain words

  • Earth moving: the least work (mass times distance) to reshape one pile into the other.
  • On a line, sort and match; the cost is the area between the two cumulative curves.
  • Unlike KL and JS it keeps growing as two distributions move apart, so it always points the way.
  • It is measured in the units of the variable, dollars, kilometres or seconds.

Two Gaussians: W1, W2, KL and JS as you move them apart

$p=N(0,\sigma_p^2)$ and $q=N(\mu,\sigma_q^2)$. Slide the mean shift and watch JS saturate at $\ln2$ while the Wasserstein distance keeps growing.

Where it is used

Statistics

Two-sample comparison, energy distance, robust estimation in a Wasserstein ball, distribution barycentres.

Data science

Interpretable drift monitoring, optimal transport for allocation and logistics, earth mover's distance between histograms.

ML and AI

WGAN and WGAN-GP, the FID score, domain adaptation, Sinkhorn and sliced Wasserstein, flow matching.
