0a_04 Conditional Probability and Independence

Probability after you learn something: shrinking the sample space, the multiplication rule, and testing independence.

Key ideas

$P(A\mid B)=\dfrac{P(A\cap B)}{P(B)}$ (count inside $B$ only). $P(A\cap B)=P(B)P(A\mid B)$; chain: $P(A_1\cap A_2\cap A_3)=P(A_1)P(A_2\mid A_1)P(A_3\mid A_1A_2)$. Independent iff $P(A\mid B)=P(A)$. In general $P(A\mid B)\ne P(B\mid A)$. Without replacement: $\frac4{52}\cdot\frac3{51}$. Chi-square test of independence on a contingency table: expected count $=\dfrac{\text{row total}\times\text{column total}}{n}$.

In plain words

  • Shrink the world to B, then ask what fraction of it is also in A. Use the dice grid to see the grey cells disappear.
  • Two different questions: "of the mobile visitors, how many buy?" and "of the buyers, how many are mobile?" have different answers.
  • Independent means the conditional rates all equal the overall rate; the table explorer tests this with a chi-square test.
  • Monty Hall: switching wins (D-1)/D of the time; try 3, 4 and 10 doors.

Where it is used

Statistics

Conditional distributions, regression, likelihood P(data given parameter), chi-square tests of independence, odds ratios.

Data science

Conversion by segment, funnels, churn by cohort, Simpson's paradox, conditional click-through rates.

ML and AI

Classifiers estimate P(class given features), language models multiply conditionals, naive Bayes, precision and recall as conditional probabilities.
