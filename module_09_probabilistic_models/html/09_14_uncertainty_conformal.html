09_14 Uncertainty and Conformal Prediction

Aleatoric and epistemic uncertainty, calibrated intervals for any model, conformal sets for classification and what happens when the data change.

Key ideas

Predictive variance $=\sigma^2(x)+\mathrm{Var}\,\hat f(x)$. Split conformal: $\hat q=$ the $\lceil(n+1)(1-\alpha)\rceil$-th smallest calibration score, $C(x)=\hat\mu(x)\pm\hat q$; coverage $\in[1-\alpha,\,1-\alpha+\frac1{n+1}]$, given the calibration set Beta$(n+1-l,l)$, $l=\lfloor(n+1)\alpha\rfloor$. CQR: $[\hat q_{\alpha/2}-\hat Q,\hat q_{1-\alpha/2}+\hat Q]$. Classification: $\{k:1-\hat p_k\le\hat q\}$. Needs exchangeability.

In plain words

  • Two kinds of uncertainty: noise in the world (never goes away) and lack of data (shrinks like $1/\sqrt n$).
  • Conformal prediction measures how wrong the model was on held-out cases and uses the 90th percentile of those errors as the half-width: right for any model.
  • The guarantee is on average over test points, not in every region: check coverage by region, or use an adaptive score (CQR).
  • If the world changes the promise breaks; monitor coverage and recalibrate.

Split conformal prediction in your browser

Data: $y=\sin3x+0.5x+\sigma(x)\varepsilon$ with noise SD $\sigma(x)=0.15+0.35x$ (growing with $x$). A degree-5 polynomial is fitted on 300 points, the threshold $\hat q$ is the $\lceil(n+1)(1-\alpha)\rceil$-th smallest absolute residual of $n$ calibration points, and the band $\hat\mu\pm\hat q$ is evaluated on 4,000 test points.

Worked example: how variable is the coverage?

For any model, the coverage of split conformal given the calibration set is Beta$(n+1-l,\ l)$ with $l=\lfloor(n+1)\alpha\rfloor$. Draw 3,000 calibration sets (the scores can be taken uniform, only ranks matter), compute the exact coverage of each and compare the histogram with the Beta density.

3D view: width of the $(1-\alpha)$ interval over $(x,\alpha)$

For the true model the interval is $f(x)\pm z_{1-\alpha/2}\sigma(x)$: wider where the noise is larger and for higher confidence. Plotly is loaded from a CDN only on this page.

Where it is used

Statistics

Prediction and tolerance intervals, rank-based nonparametric inference, quantile regression, heteroscedastic regression.

Data science

Forecasts and prices with honest intervals, risk limits, coverage monitoring after deployment.

ML and AI

Deep-model uncertainty with conformal calibration, set-valued medical predictions, conformal language modelling, drift detection.
