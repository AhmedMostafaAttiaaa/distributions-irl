02_09 Von Mises

The Normal of the circle: directions, times of day and phases.

Key ideas

$$f(\theta)=\frac{e^{\kappa\cos(\theta-\mu)}}{2\pi I_0(\kappa)},\qquad\bar R=\frac{I_1(\kappa)}{I_0(\kappa)},\qquad\text{circular variance}=1-\bar R.$$ Circular mean $=\operatorname{atan2}(\bar S,\bar C)$ with $\bar C=\overline{\cos\theta_i}$, $\bar S=\overline{\sin\theta_i}$. For large $\kappa$ it is Normal with variance $1/\kappa$; $\kappa=0$ is uniform.

In plain words

  • Never average angles directly. 350 and 10 degrees are 20 degrees apart, but their ordinary mean is 180. Average the unit vectors (cos, sin) instead: that is the circular mean.
  • κ is concentration. 0 means any direction is equally likely; large κ means a tight cluster around the mean direction (like 1/variance).
  • R between 0 and 1 says how well the directions agree. A short resultant (R near 0) means there is no real preferred direction (Rayleigh test, Example 3).

Where it is used

Statistics

Directional statistics (wind, bearings, circadian phase), Rayleigh and Watson tests, circular regression.

Data science

Time-of-day and seasonality features (sin and cos), headings and bearings in geospatial data, periodic anomalies.

ML and AI

Von Mises-Fisher on the sphere for embeddings, angle regression with a von Mises likelihood, cyclic features.
