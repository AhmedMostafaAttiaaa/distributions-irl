09_09 Diffusion Models: Noise as a Distribution

Destroy data with Gaussian noise in small steps, then learn to run the movie backwards: schedules, the reverse step, denoising as regression and a small model from scratch.

Key ideas

$x_t=\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}x_0+\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\varepsilon$; $\bar\alpha_t=\prod(1-\beta_s)$; SNR $=\bar\alpha/(1-\bar\alpha)$. Reverse: $q(x_{t-1}|x_t,x_0)=N(\tilde\mu_t,\tilde\beta_t)$, $\tilde\beta_t=\frac{1-\bar\alpha_{t-1}}{1-\bar\alpha_t}\beta_t$. Loss $E\|\varepsilon-\varepsilon_\theta(x_t,t)\|^2$; $\varepsilon^*=-\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\nabla\ln p_t$. Sampling: $x_{t-1}=\frac1{\sqrt{\alpha_t}}(x_t-\frac{\beta_t}{\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}}\varepsilon_\theta)+\sqrt{\tilde\beta_t}z$. Gaussian data: slope $\frac{\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha}}{\bar\alpha s^2+1-\bar\alpha}$.

In plain words

  • Destroying data with noise is easy and known exactly; one formula jumps to any noise level.
  • The network only learns to predict the added noise, i.e. to denoise at every noise level.
  • Predicting the noise is estimating the score, the arrow pointing towards denser data.
  • Sampling runs the movie backwards in many small steps, adding a little fresh noise each time.

The forward process: noising the half-moons

1,000 training points of two interleaved half-moons. Choose the schedule and the step $t$: the data are scaled down by $\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}$ and noise of variance $1-\bar\alpha_t$ is added, so the total variance stays about 1.

3D view: the optimal noise predictor for Gaussian data

For $x_0\sim N(0,s^2)$ the best noise predictor is linear in $x_t$: $\varepsilon^*=\frac{\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha}}{\bar\alpha s^2+1-\bar\alpha}x_t$. Plotly is loaded from a CDN only on this page.
