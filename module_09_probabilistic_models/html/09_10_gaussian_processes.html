09_10 Gaussian Processes: Distributions over Functions

Condition a distribution over smooth functions on data: predictions with honest error bars, hyperparameters from the marginal likelihood, Bayesian optimisation and kriging.

Key ideas

$f\sim\mathcal{GP}(m,k)$. Posterior: $\bar f_*=k_*^\top(K+\sigma_n^2I)^{-1}y$, $\mathrm{Var}=k_{**}-k_*^\top(K+\sigma_n^2I)^{-1}k_*$ (independent of $y$). $\ln p(y)=-\frac12y^\top K_y^{-1}y-\frac12\ln|K_y|-\frac n2\ln2\pi$. RBF $k=\sigma_f^2e^{-r^2/2\ell^2}$. EI $=(f_{\min}-\mu)\Phi(z)+s\varphi(z)$. Cost $O(n^3)$. Linear kernel = Bayesian linear regression.

In plain words

  • A GP is a prior over smooth functions; the kernel says how strongly nearby inputs move together.
  • The posterior is Gaussian conditioning: a prediction and an error bar at every point, wide where data are far.
  • The marginal likelihood trades fit against complexity and selects the length scale (and the kernel) without a validation set.
  • Expected improvement chooses the next experiment by balancing a low prediction against high uncertainty.

A Gaussian process in your browser

12 noisy observations of an unknown smooth function. The RBF kernel has a length scale $\ell$, a signal SD $\sigma_f$ and the observation noise $\sigma_n$. Move them and watch the posterior mean, the 95 % band and the log marginal likelihood; Best fit searches a grid for the maximum.

Worked example: Bayesian optimisation by expected improvement

Minimise an expensive function $g(x)=\sin1.5x+0.1(x-6)^2+0.24\sin4x$ on $[0,10]$. Each click evaluates $g$ at the point with the largest expected improvement under a GP ($\ell=1$, $\sigma_f=1$). The global minimum is $-1.035$ at $x=7.365$.

3D view: the kernel and its effect

The RBF kernel $k(x,x')$ over $(x,x')$: a ridge along the diagonal whose width is the length scale. Plotly is loaded from a CDN only on this page.

Where it is used

Statistics

Kriging in geostatistics, nonparametric Bayesian regression, emulators of computer experiments, smoothing splines as posterior means.

Data science

Interpolation with error bars, Bayesian optimisation and active learning, forecasting with seasonal kernels, calibrated uncertainty on small data.

ML and AI

Hyperparameter tuning, surrogate models in RL and robotics, infinite-width networks as GPs, neural processes.
