02_02 Lognormal

A positive, right-skewed variable whose logarithm is Normal: the shape of multiplicative effects.

Key ideas

$\ln X\sim N(\mu,\sigma^2)$, so $f(x)=\frac1{x\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-(\ln x-\mu)^2/(2\sigma^2)}$ for $x>0$. $$\text{median}=e^\mu,\qquad\text{mean}=e^{\mu+\sigma^2/2},\qquad\text{mode}=e^{\mu-\sigma^2},\qquad\text{mode}<\text{median}<\text{mean}.$$ $\mu,\sigma$ describe $\ln X$, not $X$. In scipy: lognorm(s=sigma, scale=exp(mu)).

In plain words

  • Sums give Normals, products give Lognormals. If a value is built by multiplying many random factors (growth rates, delays), its log is a sum, so the log is Normal.
  • Typical vs average: the long right tail pulls the mean above the median. For incomes, latencies and prices, quote the median and percentiles.
  • Quick test: take the log of your data and look for a bell shape (Q-Q plot). The sliders show how sigma controls the skew.

Where it is used

Statistics

Log-linear regression, geometric mean and SD, confidence intervals for ratios on the log scale.

Data science

Latencies, file sizes, incomes, prices: report medians and percentiles, set SLOs on p95 and p99.

ML and AI

Log-transforming skewed targets, log-uniform hyperparameter search, multiplicative noise.
