09_04 Density Estimation: Histograms, KDE, Gaussian Mixtures and EM

From a pile of numbers to a density: bandwidth, mixtures, the EM algorithm and what a fitted density is good for.

Key ideas

KDE $\hat f_h(x)=\frac1{nh}\sum K(\frac{x-x_i}h)$, $h\propto n^{-1/5}$ (Silverman $0.9\min\{s,\mathrm{IQR}/1.34\}n^{-1/5}$). GMM $\sum\pi_kN(\mu_k,\Sigma_k)$. EM: $r_{ik}\propto\pi_kN(x_i;\mu_k,\Sigma_k)$; $\pi_k=N_k/n$, $\mu_k=\frac1{N_k}\sum r_{ik}x_i$, $\Sigma_k$ weighted. $\ln p(x)=\mathcal L(q,\theta)+\mathrm{KL}(q\|p(z|x))$: EM never decreases the likelihood. BIC $=p\ln n-2\ell$.

In plain words

  • A KDE is a bump on every observation; the bump width trades noise against blurring.
  • A mixture says "a few kinds of customers, each a bell curve".
  • EM alternates two easy steps: soft-assign everyone to a segment, then re-estimate each segment from its weighted members.
  • Pick $K$ and $h$ on data you did not fit: BIC, AIC, held-out likelihood, cross-validation.

Where it is used

Statistics

Nonparametric density estimation, mixture and latent-class models, EM for missing data, AIC and BIC.

Data science

Customer segmentation with soft memberships, anomaly detection by low density, synthetic data generation.

ML and AI

Gaussian mixtures in speech and vision, the EM/ELBO structure behind VAEs, topic models, density-based novelty detection.
