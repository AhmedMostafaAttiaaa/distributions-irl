05_08 Sampling Designs

Simple, stratified, cluster and systematic samples, design effects, and weights for unequal selection.

Key ideas

SRS: $\mathrm{Var}(\bar y)=\dfrac{S^2}{n}(1-f)$. Stratified: $\sum W_h^2S_h^2/n_h$; proportional $n_h=nW_h$ gives $\frac1n\sum W_hS_h^2$; Neyman $n_h\propto W_hS_h$ gives $(\sum W_hS_h)^2/n$. Cluster: $\mathrm{deff}=1+(K-1)\rho$, $n_{\text{eff}}=n/\mathrm{deff}$. Weights: Horvitz-Thompson $\frac1N\sum y_i/\pi_i$, Hajek $\sum w_iy_i/\sum w_i$, Kish $\mathrm{deff}_w=1+\mathrm{CV}(w)^2$. Bias from self-selection does not shrink with $n$.

In plain words

  • Stratify to remove luck: fixing the regional shares removes the between-region part of the variance; oversampling the noisy region does even better (Neyman).
  • Clusters are cheap but pay a tax: 500 interviews in 10 villages are worth about 46 independent ones.
  • Weights remove selection bias from unequal response, at the price of a larger variance.
  • Size does not cure bias: run the last demo and watch the self-selected error stop falling.

Where it is used

Statistics

Survey sampling, cluster-randomised trials, design effects, post-stratification, sample-size planning.

Data science

A/B tests randomised by user or by market, reweighting non-representative panels, monitoring samples.

ML and AI

Stratified train/test splits, group-aware cross-validation, importance weighting under dataset shift, effective sample size of correlated data.
