07_04 Thompson Sampling and Multi-Armed Bandits

Learning which option is best while using it: regret, probability matching and when a bandit beats an A/B test.

Key ideas

Regret $=\sum_t(p^*-p_{A_t})=\sum_k\Delta_kN_k$. Thompson: draw $\tilde p_k\sim\mathrm{Beta}(1+s_k,1+f_k)$, play $\arg\max_k\tilde p_k$; arm $k$ is played with probability $P(k\text{ best}\mid\text{data})$. UCB1: $\hat p_k+\sqrt{2\ln t/N_k}$. Lai-Robbins: $\liminf E[\mathrm{Regret}(T)]/\ln T\ge\sum_k\Delta_k/\mathrm{KL}(p_k\|p^*)$. Uniform split: regret $\bar\Delta T$ (linear).

In plain words

  • Show the banner that looks best, but stay curious: uncertain banners sometimes win the posterior draw and get shown.
  • The equal split loses clicks forever; compare the straight grey line with the bending regret curve.
  • A bandit earns, a test learns: with two near-equal banners the bandit loses almost nothing but cannot name a winner.
  • Stale statistics cost a little; frequent refreshes keep most of the gain.

Where it is used

Statistics

Adaptive clinical trials, sequential design, dose finding; the exploration-exploitation problem in decision theory.

Data science

Banner, headline and email optimisation, dynamic pricing, recommendation slots, bandit modes of experimentation platforms.

ML and AI

Contextual bandits for recommenders and ranking, hyper-parameter and prompt selection, exploration in reinforcement learning.
