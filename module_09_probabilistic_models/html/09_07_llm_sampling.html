09_07 How Language Models Sample: Temperature, Top-k, Top-p and the Gumbel Trick

From a vector of probabilities to a word: reshaping, truncating and sampling, with a real bigram model on the SMS corpus.

Key ideas

$p^{(T)}_j=\frac{e^{z_j/T}}{\sum_ke^{z_k/T}}$; $dH/dT=\mathrm{Var}(z)/T^3\ge0$. Truncation to a set of mass $m$ and renormalising: $\mathrm{KL}(q\|p)=-\ln m$. Gumbel-max: $\arg\max(z_k+G_k)\sim\mathrm{softmax}(z)$, $G=-\ln(-\ln U)$. Gumbel-softmax $\mathrm{softmax}((z+G)/\tau)$. Perplexity $=\exp\{-\frac1n\sum\ln p\}$. Best $T$: minimise held-out NLL.

In plain words

  • Temperature is a confidence knob: halving it squares the odds between any two words.
  • Truncation cuts the unreliable tail and renormalises; cutting 10 % of the mass costs only $-\ln0.9=0.105$ nats.
  • Top-$p$ adapts, top-$k$ does not: few words where the model is sure, many where it is not.
  • Gumbel noise + argmax is an exact sample; its softmax version gives gradients through a discrete choice.
