02_08 Student's t, Chi-square and F

Sampling distributions: how means, variances and variance ratios fluctuate from sample to sample.

Key ideas

$\chi^2_\nu=\sum_{i=1}^\nu Z_i^2$ (mean $\nu$, variance $2\nu$); $t_\nu=\dfrac{Z}{\sqrt{\chi^2_\nu/\nu}}$ (variance $\frac{\nu}{\nu-2}$); $F_{d_1,d_2}=\dfrac{\chi^2_{d_1}/d_1}{\chi^2_{d_2}/d_2}$. For Normal data: $\frac{\bar X-\mu}{s/\sqrt n}\sim t_{n-1}$, $\frac{(n-1)s^2}{\sigma^2}\sim\chi^2_{n-1}$, $\frac{s_1^2}{s_2^2}\sim F_{n_1-1,n_2-1}$. Also $Z^2\sim\chi^2_1$, $t_\nu^2\sim F_{1,\nu}$.

In plain words

  • The t is a Normal with an uncertain scale. When the SD is estimated from few values, the standardized mean lands in the tails more often than a Normal says, so critical values are larger (2.78 instead of 1.96 for 4 d.f.).
  • Chi-square describes variance estimates; F compares two of them. Both are built from squared Normals, so they are positive and right-skewed.
  • With many observations all three converge to Normal-like shapes (t becomes Normal as d.f. grows). Use the coverage button in Example 1 to see why small samples need the t.

Where it is used

Statistics

t-tests and intervals for means, chi-square tests (variance, goodness of fit, independence), F-tests and ANOVA.

Data science

A/B tests on continuous metrics (Welch t), nested model comparison, quality-control variance charts.

ML and AI

Student-t likelihoods and priors for robust models, t-SNE kernel, Bayesian predictive distributions.
