05_01 The Law of Large Numbers

Averages settle on the true mean at the square-root speed, unless the mean does not exist.

Key ideas

Weak law: $P(|\bar X_n-\mu|>\varepsilon)\to0$. Strong law: $\bar X_n\to\mu$ with probability 1. Speed: $\mathrm{sd}(\bar X_n)=\sigma/\sqrt n$. Chebyshev: $P(|\bar X_n-\mu|\ge\varepsilon)\le\frac{\sigma^2}{n\varepsilon^2}$ (any shape); Hoeffding: $\le2e^{-2n\varepsilon^2/(b-a)^2}$ (bounded values); Normal rule: $\approx2(1-\Phi(\varepsilon\sqrt n/\sigma))$. Required $n$: Chebyshev $\frac{\sigma^2}{\delta\varepsilon^2}$, Hoeffding $\frac{(b-a)^2\ln(2/\delta)}{2\varepsilon^2}$, Normal $\big(\frac{z\sigma}{\varepsilon}\big)^2$. It fails when there is no mean (Cauchy).

In plain words

  • Averages converge, sums do not. The relative frequency of heads goes to one half even though the surplus of heads keeps wandering away.
  • Square-root speed. Ten times more precision needs a hundred times more data (Example 2: halving a poll margin costs four times the respondents).
  • Chebyshev is a guarantee, not a forecast. It is safe for any distribution but asks for far more data than the realistic Normal rule (Example 1: about 100 times more spins).
  • Try the Cauchy parent: the average never settles, because there is no mean to settle on.

Where it is used

Statistics

Consistency of estimators, frequentist probability, sample sizes for polls and trials.

Data science

Conversion rates, A/B tests and Monte Carlo estimates that converge at the 1/sqrt(n) rate.

ML and AI

Training loss as an average over examples, mini-batch gradient noise, averaging episodes in reinforcement learning.
