09_06 The Randomness Inside Neural Networks

Initialisation, dropout, label smoothing, minibatch noise and seeds: the distributions that decide whether a network trains and how sure it is.

Key ideas

Layer variance: $q_l=n\sigma_w^2q_{l-1}$ (tanh/linear); ReLU: $q_l=\frac12n\sigma_w^2q_{l-1}$. LeCun $\sigma_w^2=1/n_{\mathrm{in}}$, Xavier $2/(n_{\mathrm{in}}+n_{\mathrm{out}})$, He $2/n_{\mathrm{in}}$. Inverted dropout: mean $h$, variance $h^2\frac p{1-p}$. Label smoothing target $(1-\varepsilon)y+\varepsilon/K$; optimal logit gap $\ln\frac{1-\varepsilon+\varepsilon/K}{\varepsilon/K}$. Minibatch gradient SD $\sigma/\sqrt B$.

In plain words

  • Each layer multiplies the signal's variance by (width x weight variance); anything but 1 compounds exponentially with depth.
  • ReLU throws away half the signal, so He initialisation uses weights $\sqrt2$ larger.
  • Dropout keeps the average and adds noise; MC dropout shows noise sensitivity, not distance from the data.
  • Seeds and minibatches add noise too: report the mean and the spread over several runs.

Signal propagation: does the signal survive your depth?

A random network with Gaussian weights of variance $s^2c/n$ ($c=1$ for tanh, $c=2$ for ReLU: $s=1$ is LeCun/He). The chart shows the RMS of the activations layer by layer (log scale), starting from standard-normal inputs.

Worked example: dropout, label smoothing and minibatch noise

For a linear stack the variance after $L$ layers is $s^{2L}$: $\log_{10}$ of it is a plane through the origin along $s=1$ that tilts steeply on either side. Plotly is loaded from a CDN only on this page.

Where it is used

Statistics

Monte-Carlo thinking, bootstrap and ensembles, stochastic approximation and variance of gradient estimators.

Data science

Reproducibility with seeds, uncertainty from ensembles, tuning regularisation by cross-validation.

ML and AI

Initialisation recipes, dropout, MC dropout and deep ensembles, label smoothing, batch-size scaling, normalisation and residual connections.
