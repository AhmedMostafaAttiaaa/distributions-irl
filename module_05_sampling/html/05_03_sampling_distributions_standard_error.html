05_03 Sampling Distributions and the Standard Error

How much would an estimate change if we collected the data again?

Key ideas

A statistic is random; its distribution over repeated samples is the sampling distribution and its standard deviation is the standard error. $\mathrm{SE}(\bar X)=\sigma/\sqrt n$, $\mathrm{SE}(\hat p)=\sqrt{p(1-p)/n}$, difference of means $\sqrt{\sigma_1^2/n_1+\sigma_2^2/n_2}$, median (Normal data) $\approx1.2533\,\sigma/\sqrt n$; without replacement multiply by $\sqrt{(N-n)/(N-1)}$. Normal data: $(n-1)s^2/\sigma^2\sim\chi^2_{n-1}$ and $(\bar X-\mu)/(s/\sqrt n)\sim t_{n-1}$. Interval: $\bar x\pm t_{n-1,0.975}\,s/\sqrt n$; the 95 % describes the procedure.

In plain words

  • Standard deviation is about the data; standard error is about the estimate. Only the second shrinks as you collect more.
  • Different statistics, different sampling distributions: switch the lab between mean, median, standard deviation and maximum.
  • 95 % belongs to the method. In the dance, about 3 of 60 intervals miss; with 1.96 and n = 10 more of them do.
  • The difference of two groups needs its own standard error (Example 3): 11 seconds is only about 1.5 standard errors.

Where it is used

Statistics

Confidence intervals and tests are statements about sampling distributions; survey sampling and quality control.

Data science

Error bars on dashboards, A/B test denominators, sample-size planning from a target standard error.

ML and AI

Standard error of a test metric, variation across training seeds, comparing models with overlapping uncertainty.
