06_01 Estimation: Method of Moments and Maximum Likelihood

From data to a best guess for the parameter, and how wrong the guess can be.

Key ideas

$\mathrm{MSE}=\mathrm{Var}+\mathrm{Bias}^2$. Method of moments: match $E[X^j]$ to the sample moments. MLE: $\hat\theta=\arg\max\sum\ln f(x_i;\theta)$, $\ell'(\hat\theta)=0$. Fisher information $I(\theta)=-E[\ell_1'']$; $\mathrm{SE}\approx1/\sqrt{nI}$; Cramér-Rao: $\mathrm{Var}\ge1/(nI)$. Exponential: $\hat\lambda=1/\bar x$, $\mathrm{SE}=\lambda/\sqrt n$, $E[\hat\lambda]=\frac n{n-1}\lambda$, exact interval from $2\lambda\sum x_i\sim\chi^2_{2n}$. Uniform$(0,N)$: MLE $=\max$ (biased), unbiased $\frac{n+1}n\max$.

In plain words

  • Likelihood = how probable the data are for each possible parameter. The MLE is the top of that curve; the sharper the peak, the smaller the standard error.
  • Estimators are random: a different sample gives a different estimate, so always quote the standard error or an interval.
  • Unbiased is not the same as best: in the tank contest the biased maximum is steadier than the unbiased twice-the-mean.
  • The 3D hill is the log-likelihood: edit the data and see the peak move and narrow as the sample grows.

Maximum likelihood for an Exponential rate

$n$ lifetimes with total observation time $\sum x_i$. The MLE of the rate is $n/\sum x_i$; the exact interval uses $2\lambda\sum x_i\sim\chi^2_{2n}$.

Where it is used

Statistics

Fitting every parametric model; standard errors, intervals and likelihood-ratio tests; method of moments as a starting value.

Data science

Failure and conversion rates with their uncertainty; capture-recapture and 'how many are there' estimates.

ML and AI

Least squares and cross-entropy as maximum likelihood; Fisher information in natural gradients; regularisation as a prior (Module 07).
