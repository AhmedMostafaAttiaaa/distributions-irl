07_02 Credible Intervals, Posterior Prediction and Bayesian A/B Decisions

Reading the posterior: intervals, predictions for new data, and decisions with probabilities and expected loss.

Key ideas

Credible interval: $P(l\le\theta\le u\mid x)=1-\alpha$; HDI = shortest such interval. Predictive: $p(\tilde x\mid x)=\int p(\tilde x\mid\theta)p(\theta\mid x)d\theta$; Beta-Binomial$(m,a',b')$, variance $E[m\theta(1-\theta)]+m^2\mathrm{Var}(\theta)$. A/B: $D=\theta_B-\theta_A$; $P(B>A)$, ROPE $P(|D|<\epsilon)$, expected loss of B $=E[\max(-D,0)]$, $P(\text{best})$ by Monte Carlo. Large samples: $P(B

In plain words

  • A credible interval is what people think a confidence interval is: the parameter lies in it with the stated probability.
  • Predictions must include what we do not know about the rate: compare the bars with the plug-in line.
  • A probability is not a size: look at the ROPE and the expected loss before shipping.
  • With lots of data the Bayesian and the frequentist answers agree.

Where it is used

Statistics

Posterior predictive probabilities in clinical trials, forecasting and reliability, decision analysis with loss functions.

Data science

Bayesian A/B testing platforms: probability of direction, expected loss, ROPE and probability of being best; ranking items with few observations.

ML and AI

Predictive uncertainty of models (ensembles, dropout, Bayesian networks) and the allocation rule of Thompson sampling.
