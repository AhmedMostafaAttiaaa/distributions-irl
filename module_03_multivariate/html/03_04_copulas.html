03_04 Copulas: Dependence Separate from the Marginals

Glue any marginal distributions together with a dependence structure.

Key ideas

Sklar: $H(x,y)=C(F(x),G(y))$. Gaussian copula: $C_\rho(u,v)=\Phi_\rho(\Phi^{-1}(u),\Phi^{-1}(v))$, Kendall $\tau=\frac2\pi\arcsin\rho$, Spearman $\rho_S=\frac6\pi\arcsin\frac\rho2$, no tail dependence. Sampling: draw $(U,V)$ from the copula, then $X=F^{-1}(U)$, $Y=G^{-1}(V)$. The main chart shows the conditional density of $V$ given $U=u$ under a Gaussian copula.

In plain words

  • A copula is the dependence on its own. Convert each variable to ranks between 0 and 1 and look at how the ranks go together; the marginals can be anything.
  • Same correlation does not mean the same risk. In Example 1 compare the Gaussian and the t copula: the t copula puts more points in both corners (joint crashes).
  • Rank correlations are copula properties; Pearson correlation changes with the marginals (Example 2).

Where it is used

Statistics

Dependence measures independent of the marginals (Kendall, Spearman), semiparametric copula models.

Data science

Joint default and portfolio loss models, stress testing, tabular synthetic-data generators.

ML and AI

Copula-based generative models, normalizing flows (marginals versus dependence), multivariate uncertainty.
