09_12 Priors as Regularisers: Ridge, Lasso and MAP

Ridge is a Gaussian prior and lasso a Laplace prior: shrinkage formulas, the full posterior, choosing lambda, weight decay and early stopping.

Key ideas

Ridge $=$ MAP with $N(0,\tau^2)$: $\lambda=\sigma^2/\tau^2$, $\hat w=(X^\top X+\lambda I)^{-1}X^\top y$, posterior $N(\hat w,\sigma^2(X^\top X+\lambda I)^{-1})$. Lasso $=$ MAP with Laplace$(0,b)$: $\lambda=\sigma^2/b$. Orthonormal design: ridge $z/(1+\lambda)$, lasso $\mathrm{sign}(z)(|z|-\lambda)_+$. Ridge MSE $\frac{\lambda^2w^2+\sigma^2}{(1+\lambda)^2}$. Evidence $y\sim N(0,\sigma^2I+\tau^2XX^\top)$. Weight decay: $w\leftarrow(1-\eta\lambda)w-\eta\nabla$.

In plain words

  • A penalty is a prior: squared size = "coefficients are probably small", absolute size = "most are exactly zero, a few are large".
  • The penalty strength is a ratio of variances, noise over prior: noisier data or a stronger belief give more shrinkage.
  • Ridge shrinks by a fraction, lasso by an amount and sets small estimates to exactly zero.
  • Weight decay and early stopping are the same Gaussian prior in disguise.

Shrinkage rules: ridge, lasso and elastic net

Six unregularised (OLS) estimates $z_j$ from an orthonormal design. The penalty strength $\lambda$ maps each one to a shrunken estimate: ridge $z/(1+\lambda)$, lasso $\mathrm{sign}(z)(|z|-\lambda)_+$, elastic net $\mathrm{sign}(z)(|z|-\lambda_1)_+/(1+\lambda_2)$. Or convert a prior into a penalty: $\lambda_{\text{ridge}}=\sigma^2/\tau^2$, $\lambda_{\text{lasso}}=\sigma^2/b$.

Worked example: a Bayesian slope with a Gaussian prior

$n$ points $y=0.8x+\varepsilon$ ($\sigma=1$), slope prior $N(0,\tau^2)$. The posterior is $N(\hat w_{\text{ridge}},\ \sigma^2/(\sum x^2+\lambda))$ with $\lambda=\sigma^2/\tau^2$: it lies between the prior (0) and the OLS estimate, closer to OLS when there are more data or the prior is wide.

3D view: loss plus penalty for two coefficients

A correlated quadratic loss with its minimum at $(2,0.6)$ plus the penalty: the ridge surface is a smooth bowl; the lasso surface has a crease along the axes that can hold the minimum exactly on an axis. Plotly is loaded from a CDN only on this page.

Where it is used

Statistics

Penalised and Bayesian regression, shrinkage and empirical Bayes, regularised GLMs, model selection with the evidence.

Data science

Sparse feature selection, stable coefficients under collinearity, tuning by cross-validation, regularisation paths.

ML and AI

Weight decay (AdamW) in deep learning, l1 pruning and LoRA, early stopping, Bayesian neural networks, KL-penalised fine-tuning.
