00_01 Random Variables: PMF, PDF, CDF and Quantiles

Numbers produced by random experiments, and the five functions that describe them.

Key ideas

Discrete: PMF $p(x)=P(X=x)$. Continuous: PDF $f(x)$ with $P(a\le X\le b)=\int_a^bf$. Both: CDF $F(x)=P(X\le x)$, survival $S=1-F$, quantile $F^{-1}(p)$. Range probabilities: $P(a

In plain words

  • The CDF is the running total of probability. Differences of it give range probabilities; its slope is the density.
  • A quantile reads the CDF backwards: "what value leaves 90 % of the probability below it?" (use the quantile calculator below).
  • Density is not probability. The shaded area under the curve is the probability.

Where it is used

Statistics

P-values and critical values come from CDFs and quantiles; every model names its random variable.

Data science

Percentiles and SLAs (p95 latency), survival curves for churn, histograms as PDF estimates.

ML and AI

Softmax outputs are PMFs; Gaussian-noise regression defines a PDF; inverse-CDF sampling generates random numbers.
