09_11 Reinforcement Learning: Policies as Probability Distributions

Stochastic policies, the policy-gradient theorem, baselines, entropy and exploration, with REINFORCE written from scratch on a gridworld.

Key ideas

$J(\theta)=E_{\pi_\theta}[G]$; $\nabla J=E[\sum_t\nabla\ln\pi(a_t|s_t)(G_t-b(s_t))]$ (unbiased for any state baseline). Softmax score $\mathbf 1_a-\pi$; Gaussian score $(a-\mu)/\sigma^2$. Entropy: softmax $-\sum\pi\ln\pi$, Gaussian $\frac12\ln(2\pi e\sigma^2)$; bonus gradient $-\pi_a(\ln\pi_a+H)$. Reward $-(a-1)^2$: $J=-(\mu-1)^2-\sigma^2$; with $\beta\ln\sigma$: $\sigma^*=\sqrt{\beta/2}$.

In plain words

  • A policy is a distribution over actions; its randomness is how the agent explores.
  • Policy gradient: make the actions of good episodes more likely, those of bad episodes less likely.
  • A baseline costs nothing and removes noise: reward only the excess over what the state is usually worth.
  • The entropy bonus is a temperature: too little and the policy freezes early, too much and it never commits.

Policies are probability distributions

Four logits give a softmax policy over up / down / left / right: probabilities, entropy and the score vector $\mathbf 1_a-\pi$ of the chosen action. A Gaussian policy has an entropy that grows with its spread; with reward $-(a-1)^2$ its expected reward is $-(\mu-1)^2-\sigma^2$.

Where it is used

Statistics

Sequential decision theory, score-function gradients, off-policy evaluation, adaptive experiments.

Data science

Recommendation with exploration, dynamic pricing and bidding, A/B testing as a bandit, resource allocation under uncertainty.

ML and AI

RLHF and RLAIF, PPO and TRPO, robotics with Gaussian policies, game-playing agents, soft actor-critic.
