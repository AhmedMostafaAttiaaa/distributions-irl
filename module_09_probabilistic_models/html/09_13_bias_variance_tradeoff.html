09_13 The Bias-Variance Trade-off

Noise, bias and variance: why test error is U-shaped, how sample size moves the optimum, what bagging does, and where the U breaks (double descent).

Key ideas

$E[(y-\hat f(x))^2]=\sigma^2+(\bar f(x)-f(x))^2+\mathrm{Var}\,\hat f(x)$. Least squares with $d+1$ parameters: variance $\sigma^2(d+1)/n$. $k$-NN: variance $\sigma^2/k$. Bagging $B$ models: $\rho v+(1-\rho)v/B$. LOO CV: $\frac1n\sum(\frac{y_i-\hat y_i}{1-h_{ii}})^2$. Minimum-norm interpolation peaks at $p\approx n$ and descends again for $p\gg n$.

In plain words

  • Three sources of error: noise nobody can predict, bias from a model too simple, variance from a model too flexible for the data.
  • Training error always falls with complexity; test error is U-shaped.
  • More data lower the variance, so the best complexity grows with $n$; bias does not go away with data.
  • Averaging many unstable fits (bagging) cuts the variance and leaves the bias.

The truth is $\sin2\pi x$ (red). Choose the degree, the number of points and the noise; the fit (blue) uses one random sample. Press Many samples to overlay 30 fits from 30 different samples: tight but wrong for low degrees (bias), scattered for high degrees (variance).

Worked example: bias, variance and test error by simulation

For each degree 0 to 11 the page draws 200 training sets (same noise and $n$), fits the polynomial and measures at 60 test points: noise + bias$^2$ + variance = expected test error. The training error keeps falling; the test error is U-shaped.

3D view: expected test error over (degree, sample size)

Noise 0.09 + bias$^2$ (measured in the notebook, nearly independent of $n$) + variance $\sigma^2(d+1)/n$: more data lower the surface and move the best degree to the right. Plotly is loaded from a CDN only on this page.

Where it is used

Statistics

Mean squared error of every estimator, shrinkage, smoothing and degrees of freedom, cross-validation, AIC and BIC.

Data science

Choosing complexity (depth, k, degree, regularisation), learning curves, bagging and random forests, honest evaluation.

ML and AI

Deep ensembles, early stopping and weight decay as controls, double descent and scaling laws, number of diffusion steps.
