06_08 Risk, Relative Risk and Odds Ratio

A short lesson on summarising a 2x2 table: three effect measures, exact tests, and when the odds ratio exaggerates.

Key ideas

$\mathrm{RR}=p_1/p_0$, $\mathrm{OR}=\frac{ad}{bc}$, difference $p_1-p_0$, NNT $=1/|p_1-p_0|$. $\mathrm{SE}(\log\mathrm{RR})=\sqrt{\frac1a-\frac1{n_1}+\frac1c-\frac1{n_0}}$, $\mathrm{SE}(\log\mathrm{OR})=\sqrt{\frac1a+\frac1b+\frac1c+\frac1d}$. $\mathrm{OR}=\mathrm{RR}\cdot\frac{1-p_0}{1-p_1}$. Fisher: the count $a$ is Hypergeometric under $H_0$.

In plain words

  • Risk is a probability, odds are p/(1-p); for rare events they agree, for common ones the odds ratio looks bigger.
  • Give the absolute change too: a 4 % relative drop from 19 % is 122 players per lost player.
  • Small tables need the exact test.
  • Case-control studies can only give odds ratios.

Where it is used

Statistics

Clinical trials and epidemiology (RR, OR, NNT, Fisher's exact test, case-control studies).

Data science

A/B test reporting and segment comparisons of conversion or retention.

ML and AI

Logistic regression coefficients as log odds ratios; group comparisons of error rates and fairness metrics.
