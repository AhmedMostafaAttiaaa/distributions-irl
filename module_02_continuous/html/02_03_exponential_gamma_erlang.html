02_03 Exponential, Gamma and Erlang

How long until the next event, and how long until the k-th event?

Key ideas

Exponential($\lambda$): $f(x)=\lambda e^{-\lambda x}$, $F(x)=1-e^{-\lambda x}$, mean $=$ SD $=1/\lambda$, memoryless. Gamma($k,\theta$): $f(x)=\frac{x^{k-1}e^{-x/\theta}}{\Gamma(k)\theta^k}$, mean $k\theta$, variance $k\theta^2$. The time to the $k$-th event of a Poisson process is Erlang($k,\lambda$): $P(T_k\le x)=P(\text{Poisson}(\lambda x)\ge k)$.

In plain words

  • Exponential = waiting time between random events. Short waits are the most common, long waits are rare but possible. After waiting a while, the remaining wait looks exactly like the original: no memory.
  • Gamma = waiting for several events. Adding k exponential waits gives a Gamma; with k = 1 you get the Exponential, and for large k the shape becomes a bell.
  • Units matter: 12 events per hour is 0.2 per minute. scipy asks for the scale (the mean wait), not the rate.

Where it is used

Statistics

Survival and reliability analysis (constant hazard), Poisson-process tests, Gamma as a conjugate prior for a Poisson rate.

Data science

Queueing and capacity planning, time-to-event features (time since last purchase), insurance and rainfall amounts.

ML and AI

Temporal point-process models, Gamma/Exponential priors in Bayesian models, random episode termination in RL.
