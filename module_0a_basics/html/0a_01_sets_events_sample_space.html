0a_01 Outcomes, Events and the Sample Space

The language of probability: sample space, events, union, intersection, complement, and counting equally likely outcomes.

Key ideas

Sample space $S$ = all outcomes; an event is a subset of $S$. $A\cup B$: A or B; $A\cap B$: both; $A^c$: not A. Axioms: $P(A)\ge0$, $P(S)=1$, $P(A\cup B)=P(A)+P(B)$ for mutually exclusive events. Consequences: $P(A^c)=1-P(A)$ and $P(A\cup B)=P(A)+P(B)-P(A\cap B)$. Equally likely outcomes: $P(A)=|A|/|S|$. De Morgan: $(A\cup B)^c=A^c\cap B^c$.

In plain words

  • An event is a group of outcomes ("even" on a die is {2, 4, 6}); a probability is the share of the whole space that the group takes up.
  • "Or" means at least one, so outcomes in both groups would be counted twice; subtract the overlap once.
  • "Not" is the shortcut for "at least one" questions: 1 minus the chance of none.
  • Pick two events in the explorer and watch the overlap cells turn orange: they are what the addition rule subtracts.

Where it is used

Statistics

Every probability model starts with a sample space and events; p-values are probabilities of events; sampling is selecting a subset.

Data science

Filtering and segmenting data is set algebra (and, or, not); funnel and overlap analysis uses the inclusion-exclusion rule.

ML and AI

Classification outcomes (true positive = predicted and actual) are intersections of events; masks and splits are sets of rows.
