06_04 The Common Tests: t-tests, Chi-square and ANOVA

Two means, paired data, counts in tables and several means: which test to use and how to read it.

Key ideas

Welch: $t=\dfrac{\bar x_1-\bar x_2}{\sqrt{s_1^2/n_1+s_2^2/n_2}}$ with Satterthwaite $\nu$. Paired: one-sample $t$ on the differences. Chi-square: $\sum(O-E)^2/E$, $E_{ij}=\text{row}_i\text{col}_j/n$, $(r-1)(c-1)$ df; $V=\sqrt{\chi^2/(n\min(r-1,c-1))}$. ANOVA: $F=\dfrac{SSB/(k-1)}{SSW/(N-k)}\sim F_{k-1,N-k}$, $\eta^2=SSB/SST$; for two groups $F=t^2$. Cohen's $d=(\bar x_1-\bar x_2)/s_p$.

In plain words

  • Every test measures a difference in units of its own noise. Large t, large chi-square or large F mean the difference is big compared with what chance produces.
  • Use Welch by default: press the Type I error button with a small noisy group and see the pooled test reject far too often.
  • Pair when the same units are measured twice: it removes the differences between units.
  • ANOVA asks one omnibus question before any pairwise comparison; with small groups the test is often underpowered (try the power simulation).

Where it is used

Statistics

Treatment comparisons, tables of counts, regression F-tests, goodness-of-fit and quality comparisons of suppliers and machines.

Data science

A/B and A/B/n tests (Welch for revenue, chi-square for conversion), before/after comparisons, drift in categorical features.

ML and AI

Paired tests and McNemar for model comparison, ANOVA-style comparison of configurations, chi-square checks of predictions against protected attributes.
