06_09 Variance Reduction with CUPED

A short lesson: use pre-experiment data to remove noise from an A/B test and need fewer users.

Key ideas

$Y^*=Y-\theta(X-\bar X)$, $\theta^*=\frac{\mathrm{Cov}(X,Y)}{\mathrm{Var}(X)}$, $\mathrm{Var}(Y^*)=(1-\rho^2)\mathrm{Var}(Y)$. Sample size $n^*=n(1-\rho^2)$. $X$ must be measured before the assignment; the estimate stays unbiased because $E[\bar X_B-\bar X_A]=0$ under randomisation.

In plain words

  • Yesterday predicts today: remove the part of each user's outcome that their own history explains.
  • Same answer, less noise: at correlation 0.7 you need half the users.
  • Only pre-experiment covariates: adjusting for something the treatment changes biases the result.

CUPED simulator

The metric has SD 6 and mean 10; the pre-experiment metric has the stated correlation with it. Plain analysis compares group means of $Y$; CUPED compares group means of $Y-\theta(X-\bar X)$.

Where it is used

Statistics

ANCOVA, regression adjustment in randomised trials, control variates.

Data science

Online experimentation platforms: detecting small lifts with fewer users.

ML and AI

CUPAC (a model prediction as covariate), control variates in Monte Carlo and in off-policy evaluation.
