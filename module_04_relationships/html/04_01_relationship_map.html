04_01 The Family Tree of Distributions

Click any arrow to see how two distributions are related, and run the recipe live.

Key ideas

Distributions are built from each other by six mechanisms: sum (add independent copies), transformation (apply a function), limit (let a parameter grow), special case (fix a parameter), mixture (make a parameter random) and conditioning (look at a subset). Knowing the recipe tells you the story, the moments and how to simulate it.

The recipes in one line each: sum $M_{X+Y}(t)=M_X(t)M_Y(t)$; transformation $F_{g(X)}(y)=F_X(g^{-1}(y))$ and $F^{-1}(U)\sim F$; limit $\mathrm{Bin}(n,\lambda/n) o\mathrm{Poisson}(\lambda)$; mixture $\Lambda\sim\mathrm{Gamma}$, $N\mid\Lambda\sim\mathrm{Poisson}(\Lambda)\Rightarrow N\sim\mathrm{NegBin}$; conditioning $N_1\mid N_1+N_2=m\sim\mathrm{Bin}(m, frac{\lambda_1}{\lambda_1+\lambda_2})$.

In plain words

  • Arrows are recipes: the tail distribution is made from the head's ingredients. Each demo below really runs the recipe in your browser and compares it with the theory curve.
  • The distance readout is the total variation distance between the simulation and the theory (0 = identical); exact relationships stay near 0 (only simulation noise), limits shrink as the parameter grows.
  • Try: select Binomial → Poisson and increase n; select Exponential → Gamma and increase k to watch a sum turn into a bell.

Where it is used

Statistics

Deriving t, chi-square and F from Normals; choosing approximations; exact tests from conditioning.

Data science

Simulating any distribution with recipes, overdispersed counts as Gamma-Poisson, consistent event-stream models.

ML and AI

Reparameterization and sampling tricks, Dirichlet from Gammas, Student-t as a scale mixture, Beta-Binomial rates.
