05_05 Concentration Inequalities

Guarantees that hold for every sample size: Markov, Chebyshev, Chernoff, Hoeffding, Bernstein.

Key ideas

Markov: $P(X\ge a)\le E[X]/a$ for $X\ge0$. Chebyshev: $P(|\bar X-\mu|\ge\varepsilon)\le\sigma^2/(n\varepsilon^2)$. Hoeffding ($X_i\in[0,1]$): $P(|\bar X-\mu|\ge\varepsilon)\le2e^{-2n\varepsilon^2}$, so $n\ge\ln(2/\delta)/(2\varepsilon^2)$; for $K$ statements at once $n\ge\ln(2K/\delta)/(2\varepsilon^2)$. Bernstein and the KL form $e^{-n\,\mathrm{KL}(q\|p)}$ use the variance and are far better for rare events. Bandit bonus: $\sqrt{\ln(1/\delta)/(2n)}$.

In plain words

  • The CLT is approximate; these are guarantees. They hold for every $n$ and every bounded distribution, at the price of needing about twice the data.
  • Exponential beats polynomial. Chebyshev needs 50,000 test examples where Hoeffding needs 18,445 for the same promise.
  • Checking many things at once is cheap: 50 models cost only about twice the data of one model, because the union bound adds just $\ln 50$.
  • Rare events: switch to Bernstein or the KL form; try $p=0.01$ in the lab.

Bounds against the exact tail

Average of $n$ independent 0/1 values with success probability $p$. Two-sided deviation $\varepsilon$: the exact Binomial tail $P(|\bar X-p|\ge\varepsilon)$ against each guarantee.

How many observations for a guarantee?

Chebyshev and Hoeffding are guarantees (worst-case variance 1/4 unless noted); the CLT size is approximate. The last two use the true $p$, so they need no worst case.

Where it is used

Statistics

Non-asymptotic confidence intervals, guaranteed sample sizes, tail bounds for estimators.

Data science

Sizing test sets and A/B tests with a guarantee; alarm thresholds for rare events; union bounds over many metrics.

ML and AI

Generalisation bounds (PAC learning), UCB bandits and exploration bonuses, randomised algorithms and differential privacy.
