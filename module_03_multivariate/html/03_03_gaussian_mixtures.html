03_03 Gaussian Mixtures and the EM Algorithm

Data from several hidden groups: model the groups together, with soft membership.

Key ideas

$f(x)=\sum_k\pi_k\mathcal N(x\mid\mu_k,\sigma_k^2)$; mean $\sum\pi_k\mu_k$; variance = within + between: $\sum\pi_k\sigma_k^2+\sum\pi_k(\mu_k-\bar\mu)^2$. Responsibility $r_k(x)=\pi_k\mathcal N_k(x)/\sum_j\pi_j\mathcal N_j(x)$. EM alternates computing responsibilities (E) and weighted means, variances and weights (M).

In plain words

  • Two humps mean two groups. A mixture first picks a group, then draws from that group's bell. Compare the mixture curve with a single Normal of the same mean and SD (checkbox).
  • Soft membership: near the crossing point a value could belong to either group; the responsibility gives the probability.
  • EM runs live in Example 1 on the real Old Faithful data; BIC tells you how many groups the data justify.

Where it is used

Statistics

Model-based clustering, finite-mixture models for heterogeneous populations, EM for unobserved groups.

Data science

Customer segmentation, anomaly detection (low-density points), imputation, BIC to choose the number of segments.

ML and AI

GMM soft clustering, EM as a template for latent-variable models (HMMs, topic models), mixture density networks.
