0a_03 The Rules of Probability

Not, or, and, and "at least one": the addition rule, independence, the multiplication rule and reliability.

Key ideas

$P(A^c)=1-P(A)$. $P(A\cup B)=P(A)+P(B)-P(A\cap B)$. Three events: singles $-$ pairs $+$ triple. Independent: $P(A\cap B)=P(A)P(B)$. At least one of independent events: $1-\prod(1-p_i)$; with equal $p$: $1-(1-p)^n$, so $n\ge\ln(1-c)/\ln(1-p)$ for confidence $c$. Series reliability $\prod r_i$; parallel $1-\prod(1-r_i)$. Union bound: $P(\cup A_i)\le\sum P(A_i)$.

In plain words

  • "At least one" is 1 minus "none": to get none, every try must fail, and independent failures multiply.
  • Add for "or", subtract the overlap; multiply for "and" only if independent.
  • Series is weaker than its weakest part; parallel is stronger than its best part: try 1 to 10 servers in the reliability box and watch the ceiling.
  • Exclusive is not independent: use the independence checker with P(A and B) = 0.

Where it is used

Statistics

Likelihoods as products of independent probabilities, Binomial distribution, multiple testing (Bonferroni and the union bound).

Data science

Uptime and SLA reliability, funnels and overlapping segments, false-positive inflation in A/B tests with many metrics.

ML and AI

Best-of-n sampling and ensembles (at least one correct), pipeline accuracy as a product of stage accuracies, union bounds in generalisation theory.
